01 / Research directions

Questions worth understanding.

Rather than treating disciplines as isolated subjects, I want to understand the mechanisms and mathematical structures that connect them.

01

Complex systems & emergence

How do local interactions, nonlinear dynamics, and stochastic processes generate collective behaviour? When can a system be understood through effective macroscopic descriptions?

02

Neuronal dynamics

How do individual neurons and interacting networks generate collective dynamics, information processing, and potentially the building blocks of intelligence?

03

Universality & effective theories

Why can systems with different microscopic details exhibit similar macroscopic behaviour? What determines which details matter at a given scale?

04

Learning & intelligence

Can principles from statistical physics, probability, and dynamical systems help explain learning, computation, and the emergence of intelligent behaviour?

02 / Mathematical language

Tools for thinking from first principles.

The mathematics is not merely a prerequisite to get through. It is the language through which models are formulated, assumptions made explicit, and consequences derived.

Probability theory Stochastic processes Linear algebra Dynamical systems Statistical mechanics Field theory Algebra & symmetry Numerical methods

From microscopic to macroscopic

Identify the degrees of freedom, specify their interactions, derive or approximate their dynamics, and investigate the collective behaviour that follows.

From correlation to mechanism

Ask what generates an observed pattern, which assumptions are necessary, and what changes when a model is perturbed.

From models to universality

Search for robust patterns shared across systems, while identifying the conditions and limits under which those patterns hold.

05 / About this project

A work in progress.

Neurocausal is my personal scientific workspace for learning, questioning, modelling, and connecting ideas across physics, mathematics, neuroscience, and machine learning.

The goal is not simply to collect explanations, but to understand what follows from a model's assumptions, what mechanisms produce its behaviour, and where the explanation stops working.
