Dynamical systems and stability

Working note · Edit and expand as your understanding develops

A dynamical system specifies how a state changes. In continuous time, a common form is \(\dot{x}=f(x)\), where \(x\) can be a number or a vector of state variables.

Fixed points

A fixed point \(x^*\) satisfies \(f(x^*)=0\). If the system starts there, it stays there under the deterministic dynamics.

Stability

Stability asks what happens after a small perturbation away from a fixed point. Nearby trajectories may return, remain nearby, or move away. Linearizing the dynamics near a fixed point often gives a first test of local behaviour.

Why it matters

The same language helps study mechanical systems, population models, optimization dynamics, and neuronal models. A neuron model, for example, can have resting states, threshold-driven spikes, or oscillations depending on its equations and parameters.
